The Story Behind Every Canvas

Born in Amsterdam in 1979, John Van Gogh grew up surrounded by the paintings of his great-great-uncle, Vincent. His earliest memories are of standing on a wooden crate in his grandfather's studio, holding a brush too big for his hands, mesmerized by the way linseed oil carried pigment across raw linen.

After 8 years of classical training at the Royal Academy of Art, The Hague, John spent a decade living and painting in Provence — retracing the exact landscapes, wheat fields, and starry nights that inspired Vincent over a century before. What emerged was a style uniquely his: post-impressionist rigor, but looser, warmer, more intimate — painted alla prima in a single wet-on-wet sitting, letting the colors blend on the canvas instead of the palette.

Today, John works out of a brick canal-side studio in central Amsterdam. Every canvas is painted in oil on Belgian linen, varnished with archival dammar, and accompanied by a signed, wax-sealed certificate of authenticity — just as every Van Gogh work has been for four generations.
